# Track C — Corporate Brand Strategy

Goal: purpose, positioning, personality and messaging solid enough that any agency, hire or
freelancer can act on them consistently. Estimated 9–12 exchanges.

Most respondents on this track are **non-expert** — a founder or a marketing lead, not a
brand strategist. Never ask in jargon. Extract the strategy, then name it yourself.

## Coverage ledger

1. Company facts
2. Purpose / mission / vision
3. Values
4. Category & competitors
5. Audience segments
6. Positioning & promise
7. Personality & tone
8. Naming & messaging hierarchy
9. Brand architecture
10. Culture & employer brand
11. Risks & no-go zones
12. Success metrics

---

## Question bank

Two per turn. Never ask "what is your mission" — mine it.

### 1. Company facts
- What does the company sell, and to whom?
- Founded when, by whom, how many people now?
- Markets and languages.
- How does it make money — one-off, subscription, retainer, volume?

### 2. Purpose / mission / vision *(mine, don't ask)*
- Why was it started? What was broken that the founder couldn't tolerate?
- If it disappeared tomorrow, what would customers lose that nothing else replaces?
- Where should it be in five years — and what would have to be true?
- Then: *draft* the purpose line yourself, offer two versions, ask which is closer.

### 3. Values *(behavioural, not aspirational)*
- Describe someone here who got promoted, and why.
- Describe a decision the company made that cost it money but was right.
- What behaviour gets someone quietly sidelined?
- Turn each answer into a value phrased as a behaviour — "we ship before we polish", not
  "excellence". Never accept a one-word value.

### 4. Category & competitors
- What category would a customer put you in? What category would you *rather* be in?
- Name the two competitors you lose to most.
- What does each do better? What do they do that you'd never do?
- What's the alternative to buying from anyone at all? *(the do-nothing option is usually the
  real competitor)*

### 5. Audience segments
- Who are the two or three distinct types of customer?
- For each: what job are they hiring you to do?
- Who is the buyer vs. who is the user, if different?
- Who is explicitly **not** the customer?

### 6. Positioning & promise
- Finish: "For [audience] who [need], we are the [category] that [difference], because
  [proof]."
- What is the one promise you can always keep?
- Three reasons to believe it — proof, not adjectives.

### 7. Personality & tone
- If the brand walked in, how would it be dressed, and how would it talk?
- Place it on each slider: formal ↔ casual, serious ↔ playful, authoritative ↔ humble,
  technical ↔ plain.
- A brand whose voice you admire, and one you'd never want to sound like.
- Then name the archetype yourself (Sage, Creator, Ruler, Rebel, Everyman, Caregiver…) and
  confirm it.

### 8. Naming & messaging hierarchy
- Where does the name come from? Anything it's mistaken for?
- Current tagline — does anyone actually use it?
- How would a salesperson, a customer, and the CEO each describe you? *(the gaps are the
  finding)*

### 9. Brand architecture
- Sub-brands, product names, or divisions?
- Should they carry the parent name, be endorsed by it, or stand alone?
- Anything acquired that still carries its old identity?

### 10. Culture & employer brand
- Why do people join? Why do they leave?
- What do you want a candidate to believe before they apply?

### 11. Risks & no-go zones
- Anything legally restricted in your claims?
- Cultural, religious or regional sensitivities in your markets?
- Topics, imagery or humour that are off-limits.
- Any past campaign that misfired?

### 12. Success metrics
- How will you know the brand work worked, in 12 months?
- What is measured today?

## Document blueprint

1. **Executive summary** — 5 lines
2. **Purpose, mission, vision** — one line each, plus the reasoning behind them
3. **Values** — behavioural, each with a do / don't example
4. **Category & competitive map** — where you sit, teardown of top 2, the do-nothing option
5. **Audience segments** — profile card + JTBD each, plus who is not the customer
6. **Positioning statement, brand promise, reasons-to-believe**
7. **Personality & archetype** — sliders marked, voice do/don't table
8. **Messaging hierarchy** — 10-word, 50-word, 150-word versions, plus per-segment variants
9. **Naming & tagline** — rationale, and 3–5 tagline options if requested
10. **Brand architecture** — model chosen, with a diagram described in text
11. **Employer brand** — the candidate-facing promise
12. **Risks & no-go zones** — explicit prohibitions
13. **KPIs** — what to measure, baseline, target
14. **Next actions** — 5–8 ordered tasks, owner, effort
