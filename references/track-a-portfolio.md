# Track A — Personal Portfolio & Website Development

Goal: everything needed to build and write a portfolio site that wins the *specific* work
the person wants more of. Estimated 6–8 exchanges.

## Coverage ledger

Interview is not finished until all nine are `solid`.

1. Identity & positioning
2. Signature work
3. Proof
4. Audience
5. Differentiator
6. Site architecture
7. Voice & visual direction
8. Conversion
9. Practicalities

---

## Question bank

Two per turn, in ledger order. Adapt wording to `PROFILE`. Never send more than three.

### 1. Identity & positioning
- What do you do, in one sentence, said the way you'd say it to a stranger at a wedding?
  *(this becomes the hero line)*
- Of everything you're capable of, **what one kind of job do you want more of next year?**
  *(a portfolio that sells everything sells nothing)*
- What work do you currently get offered that you'd like to stop getting?
- Follow-up if vague: give three candidate one-liners built from their answer, ask which is
  closest.

### 2. Signature work
- Name the three projects you'd bet your career on. *(these become the case studies)*
- For each: what was the actual problem, and what did **you** decide that someone else
  wouldn't have? *(the decision is the story — the deliverable is not)*
- Which project taught you the most, even if it looks unimpressive?
- Do you have process material — boards, versions, stills, before/after? *(process is what
  separates a portfolio from a gallery)*

### 3. Proof
- Any numbers? Views, retention, conversion, revenue, turnaround, volume shipped.
- Named clients you're allowed to show. Any under NDA that need anonymising?
- Awards, features, competitions, selections.
- Anything a past client said that you still remember? *(verbatim testimonial beats a
  polished one)*

### 4. Audience
- Who actually signs off on hiring you — job title, company type, market?
- What is that person afraid of when they hire someone like you? *(fear drives more
  decisions than desire; answering it in the copy converts)*
- What do they type into Google or YouTube when they need you?
- Where do they find you today — referral, platform, social, search?

### 5. Differentiator
- What do you do that most people at your level can't, or won't?
- What do you deliberately refuse to do?
- If a competitor copied your whole site except one thing, what's the one thing that would
  still be yours?

### 6. Site architecture
- Which pages? *(offer: Home · Work · Case study · About · Services · Contact — pick)*
- First screen: showreel that autoplays, one hero project, or a grid?
- Case studies: full narrative, or image-led with short captions?
- Anything you want *off* the site that portfolios usually include?

### 7. Voice & visual direction
- Three words for how the site should feel in the first two seconds.
- Two or three sites you admire — and **what specifically** you'd steal from each.
- One site you'd hate to be compared to, and why. *(anti-references define the space faster
  than references)*
- Dark, light, or heavy imagery with minimal chrome?
- Non-expert phrasing: *"Walking into your site should feel like walking into — a gallery, a
  workshop, a cinema, or a studio?"*

### 8. Conversion
- The single action a visitor should take. *(one, not three)*
- Form, email, WhatsApp, or a booking link?
- Rates or ranges shown publicly, or "on request"?
- What do you want to know about them before the first call?

### 9. Practicalities
- Languages, and does it need RTL?
- Domain owned? Platform preference — Framer, Webflow, WordPress, hand-built?
- Who updates it, how often?
- Search terms worth ranking for.

## Document blueprint

1. **Executive summary** — 5 lines
2. **Positioning statement** — plus 2 alternates
3. **Target audience** — who they are, what they fear, what they search
4. **Differentiators** — 3–5, each phrased as a claim the site can defend
5. **Sitemap & page-by-page content plan** — every page: purpose, sections, the one message
6. **Case-study template** — the structure, plus one filled from a real project as the model
7. **Voice & tone guide** — with sample hero, about paragraph, and CTA copy written out
8. **Visual direction** — colour and type direction, imagery rules, references and
   anti-references, and **moodboard search terms**
9. **Conversion plan** — CTA, placement, form fields, follow-up
10. **Technical & SEO notes** — platform, languages/RTL, target terms, page titles
11. **Next actions** — 5–8 ordered tasks, owner, effort
